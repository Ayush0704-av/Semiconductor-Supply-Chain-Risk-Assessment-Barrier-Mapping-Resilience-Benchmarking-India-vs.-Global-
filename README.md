# Semiconductor Supply Chain Risk Assessment
### Barrier Mapping & Resilience Benchmarking (India vs. Global)

## Overview

This project is an independent research study identifying and structuring the key barriers affecting the **global semiconductor supply chain**, with a specific focus on India's position relative to established players like Taiwan, South Korea, and the US.

The goal was to move beyond a generic list of challenges and build a **structured, categorized supply chain risk framework** that can support risk-based decision-making — the kind of analysis relevant to sourcing strategy, vendor risk, and disruption planning in real-world supply chain operations.

## Methodology

1. **Research synthesis** — Reviewed 20+ industry reports, whitepapers, and case studies covering semiconductor manufacturing, trade policy, and **supply chain resilience**.
2. **Barrier identification** — Consolidated findings into 25 distinct barriers.
3. **Categorization** — Grouped barriers into 5 core categories, with **Supply Chain & Operational Barriers** as a central focus area:
   - Resource Barriers
   - Regulatory Compliance Barriers
   - **Supply Chain & Operational Barriers**
   - Global Conflicts & Trade Barriers
   - Innovation & Sustainability Barriers
4. **Risk classification** — Applied a 4-tier severity model (Critical / High / Medium / Low) to prioritize which barriers pose the greatest **supply chain disruption risk**.
5. **Benchmarking** — Compared India's **supply chain maturity** against Taiwan, South Korea, and the US to identify structural gaps.

## Key Findings

- **Resource barriers** (fab setup cost, infrastructure, talent gap) remain the most fundamental blockers to India building fab-grade manufacturing capacity.
- **Supply chain fragmentation** — India's ecosystem is disjointed across design, fabrication, assembly, and testing, with weak vertical integration compared to Taiwan's cluster model.
- **Geopolitical concentration risk** — Over-reliance on Taiwan (TSMC) for advanced nodes and China for rare earth elements creates acute exposure to trade and conflict shocks, directly threatening **supply chain continuity**.
- **Regulatory friction** — Slow execution of national schemes (ISM, SPECS, PLI/DLI) and lack of a single-window clearance process continue to deter long-term investment in domestic supply chain capacity.
- **Logistics gaps** — Absence of specialized, certified transport and cleanroom-grade logistics infrastructure limits India's ability to compete for high-value semiconductor cargo, a critical **supply chain operational barrier**.

## Why This Matters (Supply Chain Relevance)

This research directly maps to real-world **supply chain risk management**: identifying single points of failure, benchmarking resilience against global standards, and translating qualitative risk into a prioritization framework — the same thinking used in vendor risk assessments, sourcing strategy, and disruption planning in supply chain/operations roles.
